[00:00 – 00:52]

Doria Holbrook 0:04 Look, AVs are still going to need people to operate them, and so building a people-centric business with safety and people at the core of what we do is only going to help us be more effective as operators. Underneath every AV stack is an actual car that's got to be maintained. The challenge with AVs is that there isn't a proven residual market. So if a company that finances a vehicle wants to take a car back, there's a known value of what that car is worth. That isn't how AVs exist yet. We will get there at some point, but it's not there yet.

Doria Holbrook 0:04 无人驾驶车仍然需要人来运营。把安全和「人」放在核心去建一家以人为中心的公司，只会让我们成为更高效的运营商。每一套无人驾驶软件栈的底下都是一台真车，它必须被维护。AV 的难点在于：目前还没有一个被验证的残值市场。给车辆提供融资的公司想把车收回来时，传统车有一个已知的价值，而 AV 今天还不是这样。某个时点我们会走到那一步，但现在还没有。

[01:41 – 02:05]

Daniel Abreu Marques 1:41 Let's start with the basics. Can you give me Oro in maybe one or two sentences? How would you describe it to someone who has never heard of it?

Daniel Abreu Marques 1:41 我们从最基础的开始。你能用一两句话介绍一下 Oro 吗？如果对方完全没听说过，你会怎么描述它？

Doria Holbrook 1:49 Oro 专注服务的是「所有权、运营与调度」这一层：它把供给端和需求平台连接起来，去满足网约车以及其他出行需求——不管这些需求是由人驾驶满足，还是由无人驾驶满足。

[02:05 – 03:20]

Daniel Abreu Marques 2:05 And can you also walk me back? Who had the idea, and what was the moment inside of Hertz where somebody said we should build a separate company for this?

Daniel Abreu Marques 2:05 能不能也带我回溯一下：这个想法是谁提出来的？在赫兹内部，是什么时刻让某个人说「我们应该为这件事单独建一家公司」？

Doria Holbrook 2:15 That's a good question. I think we have been in the rideshare rental business, which is the most adjacent to Hertz's core business, for about the last 10 years. And so as we have seen the industry evolve, it became clear that there was a need for this rapid turn, mass dispatch capability that Hertz already had. We just need to apply it in a new use case. And so we launched our managed fleet business where we put Oro drivers in Oro vehicles to fulfil ride-hail demand on demand platforms about a year and a half ago in pilot, and then we decided to commercialize about a year ago now, last September. And then from there it was just a decision of would it make the most sense to commercialize under the Hertz brand name, or should we actually go launch a separate company to allow us to go even faster.

Doria Holbrook 2:15 这是个好问题。我们做网约车租赁业务大概已经有十年了，那是和赫兹核心业务最邻近的一块。随着行业演变，我们清楚地看到市场需要一种「快速周转 + 大规模调度」的能力——而这种能力赫兹本来就有，只是要把它用到新的场景里。所以我们推出了管理车队业务：Oro 的司机开 Oro 的车，在需求平台上承接网约车订单。大约一年半前先做试点，然后一年前、也就是去年九月决定商业化。接下来的问题就只是：继续挂在赫兹品牌下商业化更合适，还是干脆另起一家公司，让我们能跑得更快。

[03:20 – 04:21]

Doria Holbrook 3:20 And that's where Oro came from, because we know that innovation in a large company can be difficult, and so creating more of that independence allows us to go and break stuff and move very quickly, versus in Hertz where it would be a higher risk for the business to do the same thing. So that's really where it came from. And then in January we said should we take that one step further and one, announce it to the world, what's going on behind the scenes, and then two, should we actually go out and fundraise independently to try to get additional investment and growth capital into the business.

Doria Holbrook 3:20 Oro 就是这么来的。我们知道在大公司里做创新很难，所以多给一点独立性，就能让我们去试错、去把事情弄坏再修好，并且跑得很快；同样的事情放在赫兹体内做，对公司来说风险更高。它真正的来源就是这样。到了今年一月我们又问：要不要再往前走一步——第一，把幕后正在做的事对外公布；第二，是不是该独立去融资，为这门业务拿到额外的投资和成长资本。

Daniel Abreu Marques 3:43 As you mentioned, Hertz is a hundred-year-old rental company and building a new business inside such an established company is hard. So what have you found most challenging?

Daniel Abreu Marques 3:43 正如你提到的，赫兹是一家百年租车公司，在这样一家成熟企业里建新业务很难。你觉得最挑战的是什么？

[04:21 – 04:54]

Doria Holbrook 4:21 I think the hardest part is large companies run a lot of pilots, and so getting folks to go from "this is just another thing we have to test" to being excited about the testing and then knowing we're actually going to turn this into something — this isn't just another test that we're running. And so as we made that pivot from testing to commercialization, it was really about telling the story and winning over the hearts and minds of our people. And that came over time.

Doria Holbrook 4:21 最难的我觉得是：大公司会跑很多试点，所以要让大家从「这不过是又一个要测的东西」，转变为对测试本身感到兴奋、并且相信我们真的会把它做成事——这不是又一次跑完就存档的试验。当我们从测试转向商业化时，本质上是在讲故事、争取人心，而这是靠时间慢慢赢来的。

[04:54 – 05:48]

Doria Holbrook 4:54 The other thing that's been challenging about innovation within a large company is making build-versus-buy decisions: where do you use the existing infrastructure, where do you augment infrastructure, where do you build your own technology versus leverage the existing technology versus go and vendorize new technology. So everything across both the real estate and the technology was a conversation throughout, and I think sometimes that can be tricky conversations even within a large organization.

Doria Holbrook 4:54 在大公司里做创新的另一个难点，是做「自建还是外购」的决策：哪些地方直接用现有基础设施，哪些地方要去增强，哪些技术自己做、哪些复用公司已有的、哪些去买第三方。地产和技术这两条线上的每一个环节，全程都是讨论，而且有时候在大组织内部，这些讨论可以相当棘手。

Daniel Abreu Marques 5:09 You already mentioned that Oro is a new brand, and I probably think something like Hertz Mobility Solutions would have carried recognition. Why did you decide to name the new company Oro Mobility instead of something that connects to Hertz?

Daniel Abreu Marques 5:09 你提到 Oro 是一个新品牌。我本以为叫「Hertz Mobility Solutions」这类名字会自带认知度。为什么最后选了 Oro Mobility，而不是一个和赫兹挂钩的名字？

Doria Holbrook 5:09 Yeah, so our origin story is with Hertz. Oro is gold in Portuguese, and so we take that from our origins within Hertz, but we ended up going with a different brand, even grounded in the history that is Hertz, largely to try to continue to differentiate the products and services and capabilities that we're building within Oro versus what we would be building purely as something tangential to Hertz. And so it was really a goal to create both the connection back to our origins as well as a sign of the separation and independence that we're working towards.

Doria Holbrook 5:09 我们的起源故事在赫兹。Oro 在葡萄牙语里是「黄金」的意思，这来自我们在赫兹的出身；但最终我们还是用了另一个品牌，虽然它扎根于赫兹的历史，主要是想持续区分开：Oro 内部正在打造的产品、服务和能力，和单纯作为赫兹附属业务去做的东西不是一回事。所以目标其实是两个：既保留与出身的连接，也给出一个我们正在走向独立分离的信号。

为什么先把人类驾驶做大（pit stop vs depot）

[05:50 – 06:43]

Daniel Abreu Marques 5:50 Can you also tell me what was the market signal that told you the timing is right to go into AVs right now?

Daniel Abreu Marques 5:50 能不能也说说什么样的市场信号让你们判断，现在正是切进 AV 的时机？

Doria Holbrook 5:57 It's a great question. We're starting to see autonomous vehicles expand from testing to commercialization now, and then the question is where, with what partners, who's at what stage in the journey — because not all AV companies are at the same point in their go-to-market. And so for us, the question was not necessarily "is now the right time", but more so "how should we do it to get the timing right".

Doria Holbrook 5:57 这是个好问题。我们现在开始看到自动驾驶从测试走向商业化，接下来的问题是：在哪里、和哪些伙伴、对方处在旅程的哪个阶段——因为并不是所有 AV 公司都在同一个商业化进度上。所以对我们来说，问题不是「现在时机对不对」，而更像是「我们该怎么做，才能把时机踩准」。

[06:43 – 07:18]

Doria Holbrook 6:43 And for us, that's why you'll hear us talk a lot about our driver-led businesses, which we see as a precursor to our driverless businesses, because it allows us to build the infrastructure, the capabilities, the turn times, the SOPs, the operating rhythm of a business while you have revenue and profitability against it, and then convert that business to driverless operations over time — or I would actually say augment the business to driverless operations over time. So when you think about a driver coming in from dispatch, that vehicle needs to get cleaned, charged, turned in minutes, or less than 30 minutes, before the next set of dispatch needs to happen. That skill is the exact same one that we'll need to use when an AV is coming in: it needs to be turned quickly and dispatched quickly.

Doria Holbrook 6:43 对我们来说，这就是为什么你会经常听到我们谈「人类驾驶」这块业务，我们把它看作无人驾驶业务的前身。因为它让我们能先把基础设施、能力、周转时间、SOP、运营节奏建起来，同时这块业务本身是有收入和利润的；然后再随着时间把它转成无人驾驶运营——更准确地说，是把它扩展到无人驾驶运营。你想想，一个司机从调度进来，这辆车必须被清洁、充电、周转，时间要按分钟计，或者在下一批调度发生前少于 30 分钟完成。这项技能和 AV 进来时需要的完全一样：快速周转、快速再调度。

[07:18 – 08:03]

Doria Holbrook 7:18 The other thing that this allows us to do is that our driver-led rental business has real estate throughout 33 markets in the US, and that retail footprint we think about as our pit stops, versus our larger depots. And so we're able to build those pit stops throughout the cities that AVs could stop at for opportunistic charging, they could stop at for lost and found, for quick cleaning. And together that business today — we have 45,000 vehicles in that fleet, it's growing, it's profitable — and so we're able to start building out this network and these capabilities but with a business that exists today, that's profitable and growing today.

Doria Holbrook 7:18 这还带来另一个好处：我们这块人类驾驶的租赁业务，在美国 33 个市场都有地产。我们把这个零售网点看作「快修补给点（pit stops）」，与更大的「基地（depots）」相区分。这样我们就能在 AV 会经过的各个城市铺这些补给点——它们可以顺路充电，可以处理失物招领，可以做快速清洁。合在一起，这块业务今天有 45,000 台车，在增长，也是盈利的。所以我们是在用一个今天已经存在、今天就在盈利和增长的业务，去把这张网络和这些能力建起来。

Oro 到底卖给 AV 公司什么

[08:03 – 09:14]

Daniel Abreu Marques 8:03 Now let's get really concrete about what Oro actually sells on the AV side. Can you break down the service deck? If I'm an AV developer and I sign with Oro, what exactly am I buying, and what is still the responsibility of them?

Daniel Abreu Marques 8:03 现在我们具体一点：Oro 在 AV 这一侧到底卖的是什么？能不能拆一下服务清单——假设我是一个 AV 开发商，和 Oro 签约，我究竟买到了什么，哪些仍然是我自己的责任？

Doria Holbrook 8:15 Great question. So Oro will own, operate and orchestrate AVs. That means you can operate out of our depots, you can leverage our maintenance services, our technicians, our turn times, we can run the depots for you. And I would say the second component of this is we work with the AV partners to find out what do they want to do, what do they want partners to do, how can we support and help them scale their businesses. And so some of it is meeting different players where they are in their crawl, walk, run journey. And it's really about serving those customers in whatever they need at that point in time. So some folks need safety drivers, some folks need supervised driving, some folks just need real estate access, some folks need advice on how to do maintenance on the core vehicles. So we do all sorts of stuff, whether it's a la carte or full service, we're really here to help accelerate development of this industry.

Doria Holbrook 8:15 好问题。Oro 会拥有、运营并调度 AV。这意味着你可以从我们的基地出车，可以用我们的维保服务、我们的技师、我们的周转节奏，我们可以替你运营基地。第二块是：我们和 AV 伙伴一起搞清楚——他们想做什么、希望伙伴承担什么、我们怎么支持并帮他们把业务规模化。所以一部分工作是「按对方所处的爬、走、跑阶段去接住他」。核心就是在这个时点满足客户需要的任何东西。有的客户需要安全员，有的需要有人监督的驾驶，有的只需要场地使用权，有的需要在核心车辆维护上获得建议。所以从单项服务到全包，我们什么都做，本质上是在帮这个行业加速。

[09:14 – 10:14]

Daniel Abreu Marques 9:14 That sounds great. And in some offerings I think there is somehow a gray zone — things like remote assistance, incident response or roadside recovery. Where do you as Oro draw that line?

Daniel Abreu Marques 9:14 听起来不错。不过有些服务在我看来自带「灰色地带」，比如远程协助、事故响应、道路救援。作为 Oro，你们把线划在哪里？

Doria Holbrook 9:25 I would say remote assistance is one that we're open to. We're not doing it today. We have large teams that are capable of expanding into that. We run obviously large call centers today. Now I know that that capability is somewhat different than a roadside or a remote assistance, but those same capabilities and skills can be adapted for the remote assistance piece. We already have a huge roadside business today across not only the 45,000 vehicles in Oro's fleet, but across the 500,000 vehicles in Hertz's fleet. There's capabilities that we can leverage as we think about roadside and other capabilities. So it's actually all grounded in the fact that we have been running very large fleets through their entire life cycle for 100 years. That allows us to adapt that to AVs.

Doria Holbrook 9:25 远程协助这块我们是开放的，只是今天还没做。我们有可以扩展到这项业务的大型团队——我们目前就在运营大型呼叫中心。我知道这跟道路救援或远程协助有差别，但同样的能力和技能是可以改造过去用的。今天我们已经有一块很大的道路救援业务，不只是 Oro 车队这 45,000 台，还包括赫兹车队那 50 万台。所以在考虑道路救援和其他能力时，我们是有可复用的。归根到底，这一切成立的前提是：我们做大型车队的全生命周期运营，已经做了一百年。这让我们能把这些能力迁移到 AV 上。

[10:14 – 11:32]

Daniel Abreu Marques 10:14 Make sense. And you already explained that you have two different businesses under one roof — the driver-led side and also the autonomous fleet operations. Where are the synergies? Are those two different companies under the one roof, or is it more like one company with shared infrastructure that you can leverage for both business units?

Daniel Abreu Marques 10:14 明白了。你也提到，同一个屋檐下有两块业务：人类驾驶这一侧，以及无人驾驶车队运营。协同在哪里？它们是同一个屋檐下的两家不同公司，还是更像一家公司、共享基础设施供两个业务单元使用？

Doria Holbrook 10:35 It's a good question. I would say a little bit of both. We definitely can leverage shared infrastructure. So for example, let's say we have a vehicle that comes back that's having mechanical issues. While we could service it or outsource it to a local vendor, we could equally send it back to a Hertz depot to get maintenance and services on. That's an option depending on the type of maintenance that it needs. Let's say it's a tire change: we have the tires in stock at the airport, we could pop the vehicle over, have the tires switched out and come right back to the business, and so you can decrease your turn times in that way. So I would say there's more overlap or sharing of capabilities that we can leverage. But to be honest, we're still trying to define where those lines are between each organization, and that's part of what makes this fun and makes it an adventure — we have so many shared capabilities that we could leverage, and we're working through that over time.

Doria Holbrook 10:35 好问题，我觉得两者都有一点。我们显然可以复用共享的基础设施。举个例子：一台车回来发现有机械问题，我们可以自己修，也可以外包给本地供应商，但同样可以把它送回赫兹的基地去做维保。这取决于需要哪类维保。比如换轮胎：机场那边有轮胎库存，把车开过去换完再回到业务里，这样周转时间就降下来了。所以我会说更多是能力的重叠与共享。但说实话，我们仍在界定两个组织之间的边界在哪里，而这正是这件事有意思、像一场冒险的一部分——可复用的共享能力太多了，我们在逐步理清。

[11:32 – 12:55]

Daniel Abreu Marques 11:32 What I'm also curious to learn about: what are you actually measured on by your partners on the AV side? Is it vehicle uptime? Is it turnaround time in the depot? What's the KPI that defines whether you're doing a good job?

Daniel Abreu Marques 11:32 我还很好奇：在 AV 这一侧，伙伴们到底用什么指标衡量你？是车辆可用率？还是基地里的周转时间？判断你们干得好不好的 KPI 是哪个？

Doria Holbrook 11:45 I would say it's a suite of KPIs. Turn time is obviously one, except I would say safety is probably the key metric. And so how quickly are we responding to safety incidents, how quickly are we measuring safety, do we have the appropriate safety management system in place to address the challenges that an AV might see. And then on the operational side, yes, it's absolutely turn time. And a couple of things I'm a little limited on what I can say given our contract and our NDA, but I would say it's pretty typical of what you would see in any fleet organization. So you have both your cycle time and turning the vehicles: the cycle time on every single piece of the pie, whether it's your charging cycle time, whether it's your tire change cycle time, whether it's just maintenance downtime. Overall there's different cycle times and safety records that are required on everything. [23:55 – 24:33]

[24:33 – 25:54]

Doria Holbrook 24:33 如果一家为车辆提供融资的公司要把车收回来，传统车有一个已知的价值，而 AV 今天还不是这样。某个时点我们会走到那一步，但现在还没有。所以问题是：如果你在任意时点都没有一个已知的残值，你可能就只能用一条直线折旧曲线。真正要解决的是：我们该采用什么样的融资结构来对应这件事。
