编者按与导语

*Editor's Note & Introduction*

Thanks to Jonah Bliss for originally covering this on The Curbivore — *Let Them Linger? The Surprising Case for Robotaxis at the Curb*.

感谢 Jonah Bliss 最早在 The Curbivore 上报道过这个话题——《让它们多留一会儿？为路边的 Robotaxi 辩护的惊人论据》。

In a new study, Associate Professor Michael Hyland at UC Irvine explains why giving robotaxis somewhere to wait between rides could be crucial to avoiding unnecessary miles on city streets.

在一项新研究中，UC Irvine 副教授 Michael Hyland 解释了：为什么给 robotaxi 一个「在两单之间等待的地方」，对于避免城市街道上产生不必要的里程至关重要。

Where should a robotaxi go after dropping off a passenger? It could wait at the curb, head back to a depot or it could simply keep moving.

一辆 robotaxi 在放下乘客之后该去哪里？它可以在路边等待，可以回库，也可以就是继续往前开。

A new study from researchers at UC Irvine and UC Berkeley examines what happens when shared autonomous vehicles are prevented from resting (otherwise known as staging) at the curb in San Francisco. The researchers simulated a fleet of 1,700 robotaxis serving 68,000 daily trips. In the no-staging scenario, the fleet traveled 564,650 kilometers per day. Allowing the vehicles to stage at the curb reduced daily vehicle miles traveled (VMT) by nearly 60%.

UC Irvine 与 UC Berkeley 的研究者做了一项新研究，考察在旧金山「禁止共享自动驾驶车辆在路边歇脚（即所谓 staging）」时会发生什么。研究者模拟了一支由 1,700 辆 robotaxi 组成的车队，服务每天 68,000 次行程。在「不允许歇脚」的场景下，车队每天行驶 564,650 公里；而允许车辆在路边歇脚，可将每日车辆行驶里程（VMT）降低近 60%。

Michael Hyland, Associate Professor of Civil and Environmental Engineering at UC Irvine and one of the researchers behind the study, spoke to The Driverless Digest Editor Ben Hubbard about why curb access matters for robotaxis, how cities should manage valuable curb space, and what the rise of personally owned driverless vehicles could mean for congestion.

Michael Hyland 是 UC Irvine 土木与环境工程副教授，也是这项研究的研究者之一。他接受了 The Driverless Digest 主编 Ben Hubbard 的访谈，谈到了为什么路缘使用权对 robotaxi 很重要、城市该如何管理宝贵的路缘空间，以及「私人拥有的无人车」的兴起对拥堵可能意味着什么。

一、你的研究领域

*Tell us about your role?*

Q (EN): Tell us about your role?

Q (中文): 请介绍一下你的角色？

A (EN): My research is broadly on modeling and analyzing transportation systems. I tend to focus on innovations in the transportation space, such as driverless vehicles, both in terms of shared fleets and personally owned, but also technologies like e-scooters, e-bikes, ride-hailing and micro-transit. My work involves how to operate these systems efficiently, how to design them effectively, and then I also try to understand their impact on people living in cities.

A (中文): 我的研究广义上是围绕交通系统的建模与分析。我倾向于聚焦交通领域的各种创新，比如无人驾驶车辆——既包括共享车队，也包括私人拥有——此外还有电动滑板车、电动自行车、网约车和微公交等技术。我的工作涉及：如何高效地运营这些系统、如何有效地设计它们，同时我也试图理解它们对城市居民的影响。

二、路缘禁停实验与主要结果

*The experiment: banning shared AVs from the curb*

Q (EN): You've recently conducted a pretty comprehensive study on managing curb usage. Can you explain the experiment and what you set out to understand?

Q (中文): 你最近做了一项相当全面的、关于路缘使用管理的研究。能解释一下这个实验，以及你们想搞清楚什么吗？

A (EN): The idea was to test the impact of banning shared automated vehicles from using the curb. So this is not when they pick up and drop off passengers, but for what we call staging or parking between one drop-off and the next pickup. Essentially, the time when the vehicle doesn't have anything to do.

A (中文): 我们的想法是测试「禁止共享自动驾驶车辆使用路缘」会带来什么影响。注意，这不是指它们上下客的时候，而是指我们所说的 staging——也就是在一次下客到下一次接客之间的停放，本质上是车辆无事可做的那段时间。

A (EN): The main result, of course, is that it has pretty big implications on what these vehicles do with their time. When they can't stage, they just circle around the transportation network. In this case, they dramatically increased vehicle miles traveled on the roadway network in San Francisco. We don't model congestion in our study, but the implications for congestion, I think, are somewhat obvious directionally. It would make congestion worse, most likely in some of the most congested parts in San Francisco.

A (中文): 主要结果当然是：这对车辆如何打发时间有相当大的影响。当它们不能歇脚时，就只能在交通网络里绕圈。在本例中，旧金山路网上的车辆行驶里程因而大幅增加。我们的研究并没有对拥堵建模，但我认为对拥堵的影响在方向上是相当明显的：它会让拥堵更糟，而且最可能发生在旧金山最拥堵的区域。

A (EN): We ran some other scenarios related to weekends versus weekdays, preventing vehicles from staging at night versus only during the day. Most of the results were pretty similar. If you're strategic with where you ban the robotaxis from using the curb, the size of the VMT impact is much smaller, but there still is an adverse impact.

A (中文): 我们还跑了一些其他场景，比如周末对比工作日、禁止夜间歇脚对比仅禁止白天歇脚。大多数结果都相当接近。如果你在「禁止 robotaxi 使用路缘」的地点上更有策略性，VMT 影响的幅度会小得多，但仍然存在不利影响。

三、研究的由来：Waymo 找上门

*How the study came about*

Q (EN): Plenty of people have probably wondered where an AV goes after it has dropped off a passenger. Do they just drive around in circles? Do they wait? Could you explain how this study came about because I can imagine this is going to become a really important topic when AVs take off.

Q (中文): 很多人可能都想过：一辆自动驾驶车在放下乘客之后会去哪？它们就只是绕圈吗？还是等待？你能讲讲这项研究是怎么来的吗？因为我可以想象，当自动驾驶真正普及后，这会变成一个非常重要的话题。

A (EN): Yeah, that's right. As there are more fleets, the impact will be much larger. The study actually came from Waymo reaching out to us here at UC Irvine via Susan Shaheen's group at UC Berkeley. They knew we did modeling and they asked us to interview stakeholders in cities to understand how they're thinking about regulating the curb and using the curb for not just robotaxis but also Uber and Lyft-type vehicles, and micromobility too.

A (中文): 是的，没错。车队越多，影响就越大。这项研究实际上源于 Waymo 主动联系我们 UC Irvine——他们是通过 UC Berkeley 的 Susan Shaheen 团队找来的。他们知道我们在做建模，并请我们去访谈各城市的利益相关方，了解他们如何看待路缘的监管与使用——不只是针对 robotaxi，还包括 Uber、Lyft 这类车辆，以及微出行。

A (EN): They came to us and said, "Hey, we think it's mutually beneficial for us at Waymo and for cities for our vehicles to be able to use the curb when we're not serving passengers," as opposed to just driving around or even going a long distance back to an off-street parking lot. We co-designed the experiments. We made the final choices, of course. That's the backstory of how the study came to be.

A (中文): 他们来找我们说：「嘿，我们认为，让我们在不载客的时候能够使用路缘，对 Waymo 和对城市都是双赢的」——总好过让车一直绕圈，或者甚至跑很远回到一个离街停车场。实验是我们共同设计的，当然，最终方案由我们拍板。这就是这项研究诞生的背景故事。

四、研究独立性之辩

*Is the study independent?*

Q (EN): So is it entirely independent? When a company like Waymo approaches a university to produce a study, there's always a feeling that the results might be tailored in their favor.

Q (中文): 那这项研究是完全独立的吗？当 Waymo 这样的公司找上大学来做研究时，人们总会有一种感觉：结果可能是被量身定做成对他们有利的。

A (EN): I can see that's the impression. The main thing Waymo was pushing back on when we were writing up the report was that they said: "Well, we didn't give you any data, so don't make it seem like we did give you any data." All this data is synthetic. We got the parking data from INRIX. We got the ride-hailing trip data from a company called Replica. Waymo really didn't give us any data, which was fine with us. They gave us order-of-magnitude information about their fleet size, which is publicly available.

A (中文): 我能理解大家会有这种印象。在我们撰写报告时，Waymo 主要在澄清的一件事是：他们说「我们并没有给你们任何数据，所以别让人觉得我们给了数据」。这些数据全都是合成的：停车数据来自 INRIX，网约车行程数据来自一家叫 Replica 的公司。Waymo 其实没有给我们任何数据，这对我们来说没问题。他们只给了我们关于其车队规模的数量级信息，而那是公开可得的。

A (EN): As far as the results, I mean I hate to undercut our study, but the directional impact was kind of obvious going into the study. We knew that by banning these vehicles from using the curb, it was going to increase VMT. It was really the magnitude that was the interesting part. Was it going to be a 10% increase? 30%? We found a pretty large number, which was a 60% increase in total vehicle miles traveled with this policy. And that came directly from the results. We didn't rerun any scenarios or rerun the experiments with Waymo's input.

A (中文): 至于结果——说实话，我不太想贬低我们自己的研究，但在开题时，方向性影响其实是比较明显的。我们知道，禁止这些车使用路缘会推高 VMT。真正有意思的是量级：会是增加 10%？还是 30%？我们发现数字相当大——在这项政策下，总车辆行驶里程增加了 60%。这直接来自结果本身。我们没有为了配合 Waymo 的意见重跑任何场景或实验。

五、多出来的里程从哪来

*Where the extra miles come from*

Q (EN): Where do all those extra miles come from?

Q (中文): 那这些多出来的里程都是从哪来的？

A (EN): There are all these peaks and valleys throughout the day in terms of the demand for robotaxis. Everyone knows when the big peaks are for regular travel, right? So the morning peak, and then you have the afternoon peak. But for a service like Uber, Lyft or Waymo, there's even little peaks and valleys that occur throughout the day, and spatially these occur throughout different parts of the region.

A (中文): 一天之中，robotaxi 的需求有各种波峰与波谷。大家都知道日常出行的两大高峰——早高峰和晚高峰。但对 Uber、Lyft 或 Waymo 这样的服务来说，一天里还有更多小波峰、小波谷，而且这些波动在空间上分布于区域内不同地方。

A (EN): But when you have these vehicles out on the road and you get a little bit of a dip in demand, there's nowhere for them to go. They're not serving passengers, so they need to find somewhere to go. And if you don't allow them to use the curb, what the software we use does is just gives them short "fake" trips to take throughout the network, circling around where they expect the future demand to be. And that, as the results suggest, leads to a lot of cumulative extra miles traveled in the network.

A (中文): 但当这些车已经在路上、而需求又出现小幅回落时，它们无处可去。它们不在载客，所以必须找个地方去。而如果你不允许它们使用路缘，我们所用的软件就会给它们派一些短的「假行程」，让它们在整个路网里跑，在预测未来有需求的区域附近绕圈。正如结果所显示的，这会累积出大量额外里程。

六、回库方案：没建模，但可估算

*What about returning to the depot?*

Q (EN): Some observers might believe these vehicles would just go back to a depot. Did you measure that?

Q (中文): 有些观察者可能会想，这些车干脆就回库好了。你们测量过这种情况吗？

A (EN): Unfortunately, we didn't create scenarios around the vehicles going back to the depot. I wish we could have but we ran out of budget.

A (中文): 很遗憾，我们没有建立「车辆回库」的场景。我很希望能做，但预算用完了。

A (EN): Given that there are relatively few Waymo depots across the entire 49-square-mile San Francisco area, I suspect this would reduce VMT somewhat, but probably not by much. I'm not sure exactly how many depots Waymo has, but let's say somewhere between two and five. Whenever a vehicle became empty, it would likely have to travel a fairly long distance to reach a depot, perhaps a mile and a half on average. It would then have to travel another mile and a half or so to pick up customers once a new request came in. So I think there'd be a pretty negative impact if the vehicles were always returning to the depot as well. Maybe you could bring that 60% number down to like 40-45%.

A (中文): 考虑到在整个 49 平方英里的旧金山区域里，Waymo 的 depot 相对很少，我猜这会在一定程度上降低 VMT，但大概降不了太多。我不确定 Waymo 到底有多少个 depot，姑且说在两到五个之间吧。每当一辆车变空，它很可能得跑相当长一段距离才能到 depot——平均也许 1.5 英里；然后一旦有新订单进来，它还得再跑约 1.5 英里出来接客。所以我认为，如果车辆总是回库，同样会有相当负面的影响。也许能把那个 60% 压到 40~45% 左右。

七、要降 VMT 就得全城布点吗

*Would you need depots everywhere?*

Q (EN): To reduce the VMT, you'd need to have depots all over the city then?

Q (中文): 那么，要想降低 VMT，就得在全城到处都设 depot 了？

A (EN): Yeah, I don't think they [Waymo] are really into real estate development. They could partner with other companies, though. One thing we learned throughout the study is that the price of off-street parking is clearly time-dependent. In some parts of the city, such as commercial districts, parking might be very cheap to access at night but very expensive during the day. In residential areas, it's usually expensive no matter what, and there are rarely large enough areas for a depot to be set up.

A (中文): 是啊。我不觉得他们（Waymo）真的热衷房地产。不过他们可以和其他公司合作。我们在整项研究中了解到的一点是：离街停车的价格明显依时间而变。在城市某些区域，比如商业区，夜间停车可能非常便宜，白天却非常昂贵。而在住宅区，无论何时通常都贵，而且很少有足够大的地块能建 depot。

A (EN): Acquiring land is also expensive. I imagine they're trying to minimize the amount of land they need to acquire and be strategic about where they park their vehicles. They'd have to consider both the location and whether they'd have to pay for parking there.

A (中文): 拿地本身也很贵。我猜想他们会尽量把需要获取的土地量降到最低，并在「把车停在哪」上更有策略。他们必须同时考虑位置，以及在那里停车是否需要付费。

八、第二个重大发现：路缘生产率

*Curb productivity*

Q (EN): Were there any other major findings?

Q (中文): 还有其他重大发现吗？

A (EN): The other major finding, which Waymo liked a lot, was related to a metric we used called curb productivity.

A (中文): 另一个重大发现——Waymo 非常喜欢这个——与我们使用的一个指标有关，叫做路缘生产率（curb productivity）。

A (EN): We measure curb productivity as the number of passengers who move between the transportation system and their destination per unit of time a robotaxi occupies the curb, including time spent staging and picking up or dropping off travelers.

A (中文): 我们对「路缘生产率」的定义是：robotaxi 每占用一单位时间的路缘（包括歇脚时间与上下客时间），能在交通系统与目的地之间运送多少名乘客。

A (EN): We found that robotaxis were about eight to ten times more productive than personal vehicles in terms of how they use the curb. A personal vehicle spends about one-and-a-half hours at the curb on average, whereas a robotaxi spends eight to ten times less time there, around eight minutes per ride served on average.

A (中文): 我们发现，就路缘使用效率而言，robotaxi 比私家车高出大约 8~10 倍。私家车平均在路缘停约一个半小时，而 robotaxi 在那里的时间要少 8~10 倍——平均每服务一单约 8 分钟。

A (EN): If you're concerned about one of a city's most valuable assets, the curb, it seems that robotaxis use that asset much more productively than personal vehicles do. Personal vehicles can simply park at the curb and stay there for a very long time. Obviously, people can get out and walk around and do many things while their vehicle is parked, but you still have a very valuable piece of land being occupied by a vehicle that isn't actively serving passengers.

A (中文): 如果你关心城市最宝贵的资产之一——路缘，那么看起来 robotaxi 比私家车更高效地使用了这项资产。私家车可以就那样停在路缘、停很久。显然，人们可以下车四处走动、办很多事，但一块非常宝贵的土地，仍被一辆并非在积极服务乘客的车占据着。

A (EN): And I should say that buses are much more productive than robotaxis, which in turn are much more productive than personal vehicles. A bus is only at the curb for a very short period of time, but it can have many people getting on and off during that time.

A (中文): 我应该说明：公交车的生产率远高于 robotaxi，而 robotaxi 又远高于私家车。公交车只在路缘停留很短一段时间，但那段时间里可以有很多人上下车。

九、城市该如何管路缘

*How cities should regulate the curb*

Q (EN): What can cities do in terms of regulating the curb?

Q (中文): 在路缘监管方面，城市可以做什么？

A (EN): I'm not sure how many cities were considering banning robotaxis from using the curb, but if they were, I would say don't do that. They might be increasing congestion a bit, but I think they're providing valuable mobility to a large number of people in urban environments.

A (中文): 我不确定有多少城市在考虑禁止 robotaxi 使用路缘，但如果真有，我会说：别这么做。它们或许会稍微加剧拥堵，但我认为它们正在为城市环境中的大量人群提供有价值的出行服务。

A (EN): Moving beyond our study, I am generally in favor of having designated pick-up and drop-off zones where it is easy for robotaxi, ridehailing and taxi vehicles to get to the curb quickly, pick up a passenger quickly, and exit very quickly. So individual vehicles are not having to fight for curb space, and the vehicles are not spending much time at the curb at all. They're just getting the passenger in the vehicle and moving away quickly.

A (中文): 抛开我们的研究不谈，我总体上支持设立专门的接送区（pick-up and drop-off zones）——让 robotaxi、网约车和出租车能够快速靠边、快速接客、快速驶离。这样单辆车不必为路缘空间争抢，车辆也完全不必在路缘花太多时间，只是把乘客接上车，然后迅速离开。

A (EN): I don't think I'd be in favor of having designated staging locations for robotaxis or ride-hailing or taxi vehicles in general. But I think just allow these vehicles to use the curb for short-term parking, in the same way that private vehicles can.

A (中文): 我不认为自己会支持为 robotaxi 或网约车、出租车专门设立歇脚区（staging locations）。我认为，只需允许这些车辆像私家车一样，使用路缘做短时停放即可。

十、私人无人车对 VMT 的影响

*Personally owned driverless vehicles*

Q (EN): What about personally owned driverless vehicles? What impact could they have on VMT?

Q (中文): 那私人拥有的无人车呢？它们会对 VMT 产生什么影响？

A (EN): That's another area of research for my group. I'm not going to make a forecast as to when Waymo or Toyota or another company is going to be selling these vehicles to individual households. But let's say in 10 to 15 years they do sell them to households. I think in that scenario, we have a lot of benefits to households in terms of mobility, but we have some very big potential implications for congestion and vehicle miles traveled.

A (中文): 那是我团队的另一个研究领域。我不打算预测 Waymo、丰田或其他公司何时会开始向个人家庭销售这类车。但假设 10~15 年后他们真的卖给了家庭。我认为在那种情形下，对家庭而言在出行方面有很多好处，但对拥堵和车辆行驶里程可能存在非常重大的影响。

A (EN): What we're seeing in our modeling results is that households absolutely will have the vehicle drop off one household member at work, drive home, pick up another household member, take them to an activity, whether that's school or some after-school activity, and then drive back home empty again. In this case, a household might actually be able to own fewer vehicles because these vehicles can travel empty and deadhead back to the household. But that's going to significantly increase VMT and congestion, unless of course there are some policies to make it more expensive to drive and make it more expensive to congest the roadways. So I think that's a particularly important policy question for cities and states going forward.
