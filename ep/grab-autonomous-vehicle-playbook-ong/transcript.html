三、司机不贵也不缺，那自动驾驶解决什么？ · What autonomy actually solves for Grab

[05:30 – 06:03]

Daniel: Super interesting. And maybe let's now get started with the premise of this whole program. I think almost everywhere, the business case for AVs starts with expensive — or also scarce — drivers. But in most of your markets, drivers are not that expensive or scarce. So what is the actual problem autonomy solves for Grab?

Daniel： 很有意思。那我们来谈整个项目的前提。几乎在所有地方，自动驾驶的商业逻辑都始于「司机昂贵、甚至稀缺」。但在你们大多数市场，司机既不太贵、也不太缺。所以对 Grab 来说，自动驾驶真正要解决的是什么问题？

[06:03 – 06:43]

Dominic: Okay, so maybe I just want to take a couple of steps back first and provide a little bit of context on Southeast Asia — which, I mean, you've kind of already started off very nicely at the beginning. Southeast Asia is a really dynamic region that comprises a mix of cultures and urban and suburban areas, and you can tell this from the GDP per capita, because it ranges from 2,000 US dollars in Cambodia to over 100,000 US dollars in Singapore. So we're actually dealing with a wide spectrum of economies and cultures here. And amidst all of this, the main philosophy of Grab is: how do we deliver technology at Southeast Asian prices? So, for example, the average ride-share fare in the US is US$14. However, if you look at two-wheel motorcycle rides in Vietnam, they're about US$1 on average.

Dominic： 我想先退两步，给一点东南亚的背景——其实你开场已经讲得很好了。东南亚是一个非常动态的区域，文化多元，城市与郊区混杂；这一点从人均 GDP 就能看出来：从柬埔寨的约 2,000 美元，到新加坡的超过 10 万美元。所以我们面对的是一个跨度极大的经济体与文化谱系。而在这一切之上，Grab 的核心哲学是：怎么用东南亚的价格，交付世界级的技术。 举个例子，美国网约车的平均车费是 14 美元；但如果看越南的摩托车网约出行，平均只有大约 1 美元。

[06:43 – 07:22]

Dominic: So the question here is: to make the economics work, do we actually try to deliver a US$14 experience and have it be 1/14th of the effect? Or do we really try to deliver the entire US$14 experience at a US$1 price? And we've basically chosen to offer the best possible technology at prices that are relevant to the region — and this, you can imagine, is not an easy technical feat. Because of everything that we've done to try to deliver a world-class technology to Southeast Asia, I think people actually see us as a reliable everyday service, akin to running water — because they rely on us for their commute to work, they rely on us for food delivery, grocery delivery. I mean, we really try to meet people where they are.

Dominic： 所以这里的问题是：要让经济模型成立，我们是把「14 美元的体验」打个十四分之一折扣地交付？还是真的用 1 美元的价格，交付完整的 14 美元体验？我们选择的其实是后者——用与这个区域消费水平相匹配的价格，提供尽可能好的技术。你可以想象，这在技术上绝不是一件容易的事。正因为我们为了把世界级技术带到东南亚做了这一切，我认为人们其实把我们看成一种像自来水一样可靠的日常服务——他们依赖我们通勤上班，依赖我们点外卖、买菜。我们是真的想「在人们所在的地方，遇到他们」。

[07:22 – 08:05]

Dominic: So with all of this context in mind, you can imagine that for our business, reliability is basically P0 to us. Our mandate is really that of reliability — look at us like a public utility service, like running water. And basically, the problem that autonomy has to then solve is: how does it contribute towards this P0 priority of reliability? And actually I see autonomy as contributing to this in two different areas. First of all, by helping us solve what we call peak-hour gaps. I was looking at our 2026 data: basically, in a city — in a very densely urban city like Singapore — the demand can easily spike about four to five times during peak hours.

Dominic： 带着这个背景，你就能理解：对我们这门生意来说，可靠性基本就是 P0（最高优先级）。我们的使命其实就是可靠性——把我们看作一种公共事业服务，像自来水一样。那么自动驾驶要解决的问题就是：它如何为「可靠性」这个 P0 作出贡献？ 我认为它会从两个方向作出贡献。第一，帮我们解决所谓的「高峰缺口」。我看了我们 2026 年的数据：在一座像新加坡这样高度城市化的城市里，高峰时段的需求很容易涨到平峰的四到五倍。

[08:05 – 08:43]

Dominic: Then there's always the question of: are the drivers available and online, and are they able to serve the demand during these intense peak-hour periods? And that's where I suspect autonomy actually has a role to play — in helping smooth out what we call these peak-hour spikes, because you don't want to run a business in which it's super spiky. At the same time, I want to say that autonomy could potentially help us solve connectivity gaps as well — meaning you have situations where, let's say, there are townships that are out of the city centre, sometimes they aren't always the most popular locations for our driver-partners to want to serve. Can autonomy actually help us plug that gap? So I think in helping us solve these two problems, it basically ladders up to that end goal of reliability.

Dominic： 于是问题总是：高峰那几个小时，司机是不是在线、能不能接得住这些需求？ 我认为这正是自动驾驶可以发挥作用的地方——帮我们抹平这种「尖峰」，因为没有人想经营一门波动极其剧烈的生意。同时我也想指出，自动驾驶还可能帮我们解决「连通性缺口」：比如说，有些远离市中心的城镇（township），并不总是司机伙伴最愿意去服务的地方。自动驾驶能不能帮我们把这个缺口补上？我想，解决这两个问题，最终都会汇聚到「可靠性」这个终点。

[08:43 – 09:04]

Dominic: So for now, we're looking at autonomy to help us continue our philosophy of being the most reliable platform in the region.

Dominic： 所以现阶段，我们期望自动驾驶帮我们延续这条路——做这个区域里最可靠的平台。

四、混合模式：一张网、一个 App · The hybrid model

[09:04 – 09:20]

Daniel: This makes sense. And also, your answer to all of this is what you call a hybrid model — it's a term that's also often used in the industry. But maybe let's also define it. So what actually is the hybrid model for you? And also, maybe, what is the alternative you rejected?

Daniel： 这说得通。而你给出的答案是所谓的「混合模式」——这个词在行业里也常被使用。不过我们还是把它定义清楚：对你来说，混合模式到底是什么？以及，你们否掉的替代方案又是什么？

[09:20 – 09:49]

Dominic: Yeah. So for the hybrid model, how I define it is: it is autonomous vehicles and human drivers operating on one network, booked via one app, from the same pool of demand, but dispatched interchangeably. Meaning we have the intelligence behind the scenes on the platform determining whether or not an autonomous vehicle or a human driver would best serve a customer's request. The platform will choose between them, trip by trip.

Dominic： 好。我对混合模式的定义是：自动驾驶车辆与人类司机运行在同一张网络上，通过同一个 App 下单，共享同一个需求池，但可以被互换地调度。 也就是说，平台在背后用智能来判断：这一单到底由自动驾驶车辆接、还是由人类司机接更合适。平台是一单一单地在两者之间做选择。

[09:49 – 10:29]

Dominic: In this situation, I would say that the hybrid model is one which really encompasses the best of both, instead of forcing a choice between human and robot. And in relation to the P0 being reliability, which is what I shared earlier: the hybrid network actually offers reliability in the most practical manner, from the perspective of wanting to administer and deliver a mobility service that is truly reliable. Now, we talked about peak-hour gaps and connectivity gaps. The other thing that I wanted to raise was that, in the context of Southeast Asia, rain really contributes to an extreme variance in demand. In heavy rain situations, when there are occurrences of flash flooding, you actually see demand spiking 9 to 10 times.

Dominic： 在这个意义上，混合模式其实是把两端的好处都收进来，而不是被迫在「人」和「机器人」之间二选一。回到我刚才说的 P0——可靠性：混合网络恰恰是用最务实的方式提供可靠性，从「要运营并交付一项真正可靠的出行服务」这个角度看，就是如此。我们刚谈了高峰缺口和连通性缺口。我还想再提一点：在东南亚，降雨会造成需求的极端波动。在暴雨、出现城市内涝的时候，需求会涨到 9 到 10 倍。

[10:29 – 11:07]

Dominic: And interestingly, this kind of demand shock caused by rain is actually positively correlated with autonomous ODD contraction. And what I mean by this is: when demand spikes due to rain, you end up with autonomous vehicles that are maybe not able to support ride requests in areas of heavy rain — because the vehicles haven't been trained, or because the floods actually prohibit the smooth operation of these vehicles. So what do you do in a situation like that, when you still have to serve the customer? Because to reject the ride request means that you will end up disappointing them, and they will leave the platform.

Dominic： 而有趣的是，这种由降雨引发的需求冲击，与自动驾驶 ODD 的收缩是正相关的。我的意思是：当降雨把需求推高时，恰恰是那些区域里的自动驾驶车辆可能接不了单——因为车辆没有针对这种场景训练过，或者积水本身就让它没法顺畅运行。那你怎么办？需求还在，客人还得服务。因为拒单就意味着让用户失望，而失望的用户会离开平台。

[11:07 – 11:47]

Dominic: Now, the second reason why I feel that it also offers reliability in a very practical manner is because you don't have a situation where you're over-scaling the fleet to meet peak demand. Because, as we ascertained, in a densely urban environment, demand can sometimes be very spiky due to a variety of different reasons.
