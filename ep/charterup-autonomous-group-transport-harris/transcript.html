冷开场 · Cold open

Armir: Physical labor is a $30 trillion industry. 43% of drivers and technicians are 55 years of age or older, and eventually they're going to retire and there's going to be an even bigger shortage there, right? You're going to see significant economies of scale, and it's going to be offered to our customer at a much cheaper price. So you're going to have a more reliable service at a much cheaper price. And we think it's going to be a great situation for our customers.

Armir： 物理劳动是一门 30 万亿美元的产业。43% 的驾驶员和技师年龄在 55 岁及以上，他们终究会退休，届时的缺口只会更大。你会看到显著的规模效应，而这最终会以低得多的价格提供给我们的客户——也就是更可靠的服务，配上便宜得多的价格。我们认为这对客户来说是一件非常好的事。

Harry: Armir Harris is the founder and CEO of CharterUP, a technology platform for charter bus and shuttle transportation. Armir founded the company in 2018 after growing up working in his family's limousine business, and it's now expanding into autonomous mobility through a partnership with Holon to bring driverless shuttles to campuses, airports, and transit networks. Armir, how are you doing today? — Armir: Doing great, Harry. How are you doing? — Harry: I'm doing well, and I'm excited to dig into CharterUP today because as I alluded to in your bio, you guys have been around for a while, so you're not a new kid on the mobility block, and this is our first time chatting, so excited to dig into the business. — Armir: Likewise.

Harry： Armir Harris 是 CharterUP 的创始人兼 CEO，这是一家做包车与接驳车运输的技术平台。Armir 在 2018 年创立了这家公司——在此之前，他成长于家族的豪华车生意里。如今 CharterUP 正通过与 Holon 的合作切入自动驾驶出行，把无人接驳车带到校园、机场和公交网络。Armir，今天过得怎么样？—— Armir： 挺好的，Harry，你呢？—— Harry： 我也很好。今天特别期待聊 CharterUP，因为正如我在你的简介里提到的，你们在这个行业里已经不是新面孔了，而这又是我们第一次对话，所以很想深入聊聊这门生意。—— Armir： 我也一样。

一、CharterUP 是什么 · What is CharterUP

[01:19 – 02:57]

Harry: Awesome. So for those who may not be familiar with the brand, tell me a little bit about the business and maybe where you serve, where you're operating, and what you're doing. — Armir: Of course. So CharterUP is a digital marketplace for renting and chartering buses. We're the largest platform for buses in North America, both from a transaction standpoint, as well as a revenue standpoint. To date, we've integrated over 10,000 buses across hundreds of bus companies on our platform. We've also built software for these bus operators. Traditionally, these operators have run their business with pen and paper or very archaic, sort of light software. We're the only platform with real-time and accurate data on the bus companies, and that data is very important — data such as availability, ratings, fleets, and so on. This year, we surpassed over 50 million miles on our platform, and that's very valuable because each trip and each mile essentially strengthens our data set and improves our ability to forecast demand, pricing, route selection, operator — and really improve the overall customer experience. Furthermore, more recently, the business has evolved into being one of the leaders in the enterprise segment. So we serve more Fortune 500 companies than any other bus company in the US or Canada, by orders of magnitude. Now we're serving some of the largest shuttle operations across universities, transit agencies, airports, and the largest infrastructure projects in the country.

Harry： 太好了。那对于还不太熟悉这个品牌的朋友，先讲讲这门生意吧——你们服务谁、在哪些地方运营、具体做什么？—— Armir： 当然。CharterUP 是一个租车/包车的数字市场（digital marketplace）。我们是北美最大的巴士平台——无论是从交易量还是从营收口径看都是第一。到目前为止，我们已经把 1 万多辆巴士、数百家巴士公司整合进了平台。我们也为这些运营商开发了软件：传统上，他们是用纸笔或者非常原始、轻量的软件来经营业务的。而我们是唯一拥有巴士公司实时、准确数据的平台——这些数据非常重要，比如可用性、评分、车队情况等等。今年，我们平台上累计的里程超过了 5000 万英里，这非常有价值，因为每一单、每一英里都在强化我们的数据集，提升我们对需求预测、定价、路线选择、运营商匹配的能力，从而真正改善整体客户体验。另外，更近一些时候，我们的业务已经进化成企业级（enterprise）板块的领先者之一。我们服务的财富 500 强公司数量，比美国和加拿大任何一家巴士公司都多——而且是数量级上的领先。现在，我们在为大学、公交机构、机场以及全国最大的基建项目提供一部分规模最大的接驳运营。

二、客户从哪里来、业务板块怎么分 · Demand & Segments

[02:57 – 05:05]

Harry: Wow. So I think you're going to be happy with this — I just went to Google while you were talking. I was paying attention also, I can multitask. I typed in "rent a bus" and CharterUP came up right at the top. So I wonder, how are people finding the company? And also, what are those segments that you're having a lot of success in? And some sound like they're growing too, like the enterprise. I'm thinking… I guess, party bus. I haven't taken a party bus in a long time, but that was the last time I rented a bus. School buses. Talk a little bit about the different categories that you have the biggest footprint in. — Armir: Certainly. So we do anything that involves buses — buses, shuttles, transit. Anywhere that buses can be deployed, we serve that use case. The business originally started out as a charter platform. We have over 10,000 buses now and hundreds of bus companies. Naturally, the business evolved more and more into the enterprise segment. This year, more than 50% of our business is focused on the enterprise segment, serving some of the largest mobility projects across the country. But there's certainly more of a B2C component to the business as well. — Harry: Yeah. And so on the enterprise use case, would that be, Google shuttles, for example? Google needs a bunch of buses or shuttles for their employees, or is it special outings? What type of enterprise business is there? — Armir: Yeah. So all of the hyperscalers use us for all their projects, whether it's on their campus, or they have a large infrastructure, data center, or chip fab that's being launched. These are some of the projects of the hyperscalers. We also serve thousands of universities across the country — whether their athletic teams need to charter buses, or their on-campus shuttles. A lot of their on-campus shuttles are outsourced to third-party bus companies. So what we do is we provide the operating system, the technology, as well as we manage transportation, providing them the end-to-end solution — as well as transit agencies and airports.

Harry： 哇。我想你会喜欢这个——你刚才说话的时候我顺手谷歌了一下（我一边也在听，我可以一心多用）。我搜了「rent a bus」，CharterUP 就排在最上面。所以我很好奇：人们是怎么找到你们这家公司的？另外，你们在哪几个细分板块做得特别成功？有些听起来也在增长，比如企业级。我猜还有……派对巴士？我很久没坐过派对巴士了，但上一次租巴士就是那种。还有校车。聊聊你们覆盖最广的几个品类吧。—— Armir： 当然。凡是涉及巴士的我们都做——大巴、接驳车、公交。只要巴士能被部署的地方，那个场景我们就服务。这门生意最早是做包车平台起步的。我们现在有 1 万多辆巴士、数百家巴士公司。很自然地，业务越来越向企业级板块演进。今年，超过 50% 的业务集中在企业级板块，服务的都是全国最大的一些出行项目。当然，业务里也有相当一部分是面向 C 端的。—— Harry： 明白。那企业级这块，比如是谷歌的通勤班车吗？谷歌需要一批巴士或接驳车接送员工，还是说是一些特殊出行？具体是哪种企业业务？—— Armir： 是的。所有超大规模云厂商（hyperscalers）都在用我们做他们的各类项目——不论是园区通勤，还是他们新上线的大型基建、数据中心、芯片厂。这些都是这类客户的典型项目。我们还服务全国数千所大学——无论是运动队需要包车，还是他们的校内通勤班车。很多大学的校内班车其实是外包给第三方巴士公司的。所以我们做的是提供操作系统、技术，同时代为管理运输，给他们端到端的解决方案——包括公交机构和机场也是同样。

三、轻资产：一辆巴士都不持有 · Asset-Light Model

[05:05 – 06:03]

Harry: Oh, wow. So you guys are clearly a tech company, a marketplace. Do you own any buses or sort of just facilitate? — Armir: No, we don't own or operate any buses. We are the facilitator, but we're the only ones that are fully integrated with these bus companies. — Harry: Yeah. And I guess I imagine that's a good thing, right? Not owning any buses — what are the pros and cons of this model that you guys have picked? — Armir: Yeah, of course. So we believe that there's pros on our end. There's certainly more pros than cons. What we have done a really good job in is focusing on the technology and focusing on the end solution to the customer. And we think that the bus companies are much better off focusing on their operations — managing their drivers, managing the technicians, right? It's very difficult to do it all. And if we focus on what we're good at, it's a win-win for both sides.

Harry： 哇。所以你们显然是一家科技公司、一个市场平台。那你们自己持有巴士吗，还是只是做撮合？—— Armir： 不，我们不持有、也不运营任何一辆巴士。 我们是撮合方，但我们是唯一与这些巴士公司深度打通的平台。—— Harry： 明白。我猜这应该是件好事？不持有巴士——你们选的这套模式，好处和代价分别是什么？—— Armir： 当然。我们认为对我们而言好处更多，肯定是利大于弊。我们做得非常好的一点，是专注在技术和给客户的最终解决方案上。而我们认为巴士公司更应该专注于运营——管好司机、管好技师。要把两头都做好是非常难的。如果我们都专注做自己擅长的事，对双方就是双赢。

四、宏观判断：物理劳动是 30 万亿美元的生意 · Physical Labor as a $30T Industry

[06:03 – 08:14]

Harry: Yeah, definitely. So we're at an interesting time in the mobility landscape where obviously autonomy is all the rage. You're on an AV podcast right now. So we can talk about this shift from a marketplace to a platform. You recently announced a partnership with Holon, bringing autonomous shuttles to the market. So talk a little bit — before we dive into what CharterUP is doing in the autonomous mobility space, we'd love to know and kind of get your take on the broader landscape and see how you see that impacting your business going forward. — Armir: Yeah, of course. So I'll take a step back for a bit. In my view, physical AI right now is one of the hottest categories, and you see this — there's billions of dollars being deployed to physical AI. And really what that is, it's physical labor. Physical labor is a $30 trillion industry. So the intelligent hardware companies are going after markets where humans either don't want to do the job, humans are just not good at doing them, or there's a lot of open jobs. So what we're seeing from these auto manufacturers or OEMs — the manufacturers are pivoting from selling vehicles to essentially selling labor as a service. So that's my sort of big picture view on it. More specifically to your question: most of the autonomy conversation has really focused on making these four-seat passenger cars driverless. And we believe that one of the biggest opportunities is moving groups of people along defined, repeatable routes — because that's what level four autonomy is. And that's where these shuttle autonomous companies are at. So that could mean anything from connecting an airport terminal with a parking facility, moving employees or students around the corporate or university campus, or connecting a university to public transit. So what CharterUP's role is, is to make that technology accessible and usable within a dependable transportation network. This is not going to happen overnight. We're really building a practical pathway to introduce this incrementally.

Harry： 确实。我们现在处在出行领域一个很有意思的时间点，显然自动驾驶是最热的话题——你现在就在一档自动驾驶播客上。所以我们可以聊聊从「市场」到「平台」的这个转变。你们最近宣布了与 Holon 的合作，要把自动驾驶接驳车推向市场。所以先聊点宏观的——在我们深入 CharterUP 在自动驾驶出行领域的具体动作之前，很想听听你对更大格局的看法，以及你认为它会如何影响你们接下来的业务。—— Armir： 当然。我先退一步讲。在我看来，物理 AI（physical AI）是当下最热的赛道之一，你也看得到——有数十亿美元正在涌向物理 AI。而它的本质，其实就是物理劳动。物理劳动是一门 30 万亿美元的产业。 所以这些智能硬件公司进攻的是这样几类市场：人不愿意做的、人做不好的、或者岗位空缺很多的。于是我们看到，这些汽车制造商——也就是 OEM——正在转型：从「卖车」转向本质上「卖劳动即服务」（labor as a service）。这是我比较宏观的看法。更具体到你的问题：绝大多数关于自动驾驶的讨论，都聚焦在「把 4 座乘用车变成无人车」上。而我们认为，最大的机会之一在于「沿固定、可重复的路线运送一群人」——因为这恰恰就是 L4（四级自动驾驶）的定义，也正是这批自动驾驶接驳车公司所在的位置。 这可能意味着：把机场航站楼和停车场连起来、在企业园区或大学校园里运送员工与学生、或者把一所大学连接到公共交通。所以 CharterUP 的角色，就是让这项技术变得可及、可用，并且能嵌进一张可靠的运输网络里。 这件事不会一夜之间发生——我们真正在做的，是铺一条「渐进引入」的实际路径。

五、「固定、可重复路线」为什么是关键 · Defined, Repeatable Routes

[08:14 – 09:41]

Harry: One term that you mentioned that I think is really important to double click on: the defined and repeatable routes, right? In the context of AVs, that really makes things a lot easier, right? I think one of the biggest challenges we're seeing is that, for AVs, if you have to operate 24/7 in all different types of weather, and it can be point to point, right? Someone wants to get picked up somewhere and dropped off — no two routes are almost ever the same. Versus, like — I'm thinking airport or campus use cases — even when you can fix one endpoint, right? If everyone is going to Apple's campus and getting dropped off in the same place, maybe you have a few different pickup points, but now you have one drop-off point. I mean, that has to make things a lot easier in the context of not only autonomy, but also operations, right? Sort of just density, number of passengers per vehicle. — Armir: That's exactly right. So for us, the first step is determining whether a route is appropriate for autonomy. So you have to evaluate the road, the intersection, pedestrian activity, whether charging infrastructure — then there's a regulatory requirement, right? And then after that, after it passes all of that, then comes the testing and supervised operation, and eventually remote supervision only if the data supports it. And that's a capital IF, right? And our view at CharterUP is that human-driven and autonomous vehicles will coexist for the foreseeable future. And so that's what we're building.

Harry： 你提到的一个词我觉得非常值得展开，就是「固定且可重复的路线」——对吗？在自动驾驶的语境下，这确实让事情简单很多。我认为我们看到的最大挑战之一是：如果自动驾驶要 7×24 全天候、在各种天气下运行，而且是「点到点」的——有人要在某个地方上车、在另一个地方下车，几乎没有两条路线是一样的。相比之下，像机场或校园这种场景，即使你只能锁定一个端点也好得多。如果大家都要去苹果园区、在同一个地方下车——也许上车点有好几个，但至少下车点只有一个。我的意思是，这对自动驾驶、对运营来说，都会容易得多吧？比如密度、单车载客数这些。—— Armir： 完全正确。 对我们来说，第一步是判断一条路线是否适合自动驾驶。 你要评估路面、交叉口、行人活动、是否有充电基础设施，然后还有监管要求。这些都通过之后，才进入测试与有人监督的运营，而只有在数据支持的情况下，才会走到纯远程监督——他特别强调，这是一个带条件的「如果」（capital IF）。而 CharterUP 的观点是：有人驾驶与自动驾驶车辆会在可预见的未来长期共存。我们正在建的就是这个。

六、最先落地的垂直场景 · Where Autonomy Lands First

[09:41 – 10:44]

Harry: Got it. So when you look out at the different business lines that you have and the different routes that you are doing, where do you see kind of the biggest impact of autonomy initially? What types of routes or what types of verticals are you kind of focusing on? — Armir: So we're focused on group mobility. So you can think of this as 10-passenger vehicles or higher, right? And this has to be a predictable environment and conditions that are understandable. So think about, like, closed loop environments — airports, university campuses, corporate campuses, industrial facilities — and then other, think of it as repeatable corridors. We do a lot of large-scale industrial projects. These autonomous vehicles are nowhere close to being able to drive on a gold mine or mining facility. This is where we're seeing and we're deploying our first autonomous shuttles. One is an airport, and the other one is a community for a transit agency.

Harry： 明白了。那么当你审视自己的各条业务线和在跑的各类路线时，你认为自动驾驶最初会带来最大影响的是哪里？你们重点在盯哪些类型的路线或垂直场景？—— Armir： 我们专注在成组出行（group mobility）上，你可以把它理解为 10 座及以上的车辆。而且这必须发生在可预测、条件可理解的环境里。所以可以想成封闭环境——机场、大学校园、企业园区、工业设施，以及其他这类可重复走廊。我们做很多大规模工业项目。（但）这些自动驾驶车离能在金矿或采矿设施里行驶还差得远。 我们目前正在部署首批自动驾驶接驳车，一个是机场，另一个是给公交机构服务的社区线路。

七、机场场景长什么样 · The Airport Use Case

[10:45 – 11:00]
